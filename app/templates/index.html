How to read this map

The grey layer shows weirdness, a noise value Minecraft uses to shape terrain. Slide the biome overlay to 0 to see it on its own. Hover or tap a spot to read its exact value.

WeirdnessOn the mapTerrain it favours
About 0Mid greyValleys
About ±0.67Fairly light or darkThe highest peaks
About ±1Very light or darkMiddling terrain

Light and dark shape terrain in the same way, because only the distance from 0 sets the height. The sign can change which biome variant appears.

Weirdness is a tendency, not a height. How far inland a spot is (continentalness) and how flat it is (erosion) also decide height, so a peak value near the sea may not become a mountain. Use the height overlay to see the result.

Surface and Underground switch the biome overlay between what's on top of the land and what's beneath it. Underground only colours places with Lush Caves, Dripstone Caves or Deep Dark, and shows the deepest one where they stack. Green is Lush Caves, brown is Dripstone Caves and dark blue is Deep Dark. Hover or tap a spot to read both.

Height overlay colours the land by approximate height: blue below sea level, then green, tan, brown and grey, up to white at about y=220 and above. The colours mean the same on every seed. It comes from an estimate, so expect real heights to differ.

Peak terrain % is the share of the central 4096×4096 blocks close to the peak value, so it's the number to compare for dramatic terrain. Average weirdness is usually near 0 because the highs and lows cancel out, so it says little about a seed.

Score rewards structures and rare biomes near spawn, the spawn biome, biome variety and peak terrain. It only looks at the central 4096×4096 blocks, while the map shows 6144×6144.
